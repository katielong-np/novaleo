## Part 1
### THE EXPLANATION GAP
*Why your labs keep coming back “normal” when you clearly are not.*

There is a sentence almost every woman I work with has heard before she gets to me.
*“Everything looks normal.”*

Sometimes it is delivered kindly with a small smile, as reassurance. Sometimes it is delivered briskly, on the way to the next patient. Sometimes it is delivered with the suggestion that perhaps she should consider an antidepressant, sleep more, “be patient with the process,” or watch what she eats. Sometimes it is delivered with no follow-up at all.

The phrase *“everything looks normal”* is, in a very specific way, true. The labs that have been run on you have come back inside the reference ranges they were designed to measure. By that measure, you are normal. By another measure, the only one that matters, which is how you feel in your own body, you are anything but.

This is what I call **the explanation gap**.

The distance between what your labs are saying and what your body is doing. Almost every woman who finds her way to my practice is living inside that gap, and the most exhausting part of being there is not the symptoms themselves but the silence around them. The lack of a story that can hold what she is experiencing. The slow erosion of trust in her own body when no one in a white coat can tell her what is wrong.

**I want to explain why this gap exists.** Because once you understand it, the entire frame you have been holding about your body begins to shift.

### Conventional labs were built to answer one question.
*The labs your primary care doctor orders, the standard panel, the CBC, the basic metabolic panel, maybe a TSH, occasionally a vitamin D, were designed to answer a single question.*

The question is: Are you sick yet?

Conventional medicine, as it exists today, is largely built around the diagnosis and treatment of acute disease. The bloodwork is built to match. It flags pathology once disease has fully developed.

It is not built to detect the slow drift of an imbalanced system in the years and decades before disease shows up. The years and decades, in other words, when you are not sick yet but you are not well, either.

There is a name for that long middle. It is called dysfunction. And it is where almost all of your symptoms live.

### The reference range problem
There is a second issue, and it is the one almost no one will tell you about.

The “normal” ranges your labs are measured against are built on a statistical average of the population being tested. That population is largely an unwell population. People who, for the most part, came in because something was already off. Reference ranges are not designed around what *optimal* looks like. They are designed around what is commonly seen.

What this means in practice is that by the time something on your bloodwork goes “out of range,” you are not at the beginning of a problem. You are deep inside one. Your thyroid hasn’t just started underperforming, it has been underperforming for years. Your blood sugar regulation hasn’t just become a concern, it has been quietly drifting for as long as you have been waking up at 2 a.m. wondering why you can’t sleep.

A functional practitioner reads those same labs differently. We use functional ranges, which are narrower and anchored to what optimal physiology actually looks like in a healthy human, not what is commonly seen in a partially unwell one. A TSH of 3.5, which most doctors will call normal, sits firmly inside the functional range that flags early thyroid imbalance.

A fasting glucose of 99 falls comfortably into the “we’ll watch it” category, when functionally it is already telling us something about your insulin sensitivity that is well worth knowing.

The labs that have been run on you have not necessarily been wrong. They have been incomplete, and they have been read against the wrong yardstick.

### The fifteen-minute appointment.
Even when the labs are read accurately, the structure of the appointment was never built to do what you need.

Fifteen minutes is enough time to assess for one specific complaint and either prescribe for it or refer you out. It is not enough time to listen, in any real way, to a woman whose body has been incrementally drifting for years and who has six interconnected complaints she is trying to find words for. It is not enough time to take a full history. It is not enough time to run, interpret, and contextualize multi-system data. It is not enough time to ask the question that actually opens the door, what does your body feel like when you wake up in the morning? Then sit with the answer.

You are not failing the appointment. The appointment is failing you. Fifteen minutes was never going to be enough for the complexity of what your body is carrying.

### A different question, and a different category of testing.
Functional medicine asks a different question altogether.

It does not ask, Are you sick yet? It asks, Why don’t you feel like yourself anymore?

It does not look at your body system by system in isolation. Your thyroid, then your adrenals, then your gut, then your hormones, as if each one operated on its own internal logic.

It looks at your body as a single interconnected system, where your hormones cannot be in balance unless your gut is, where your gut cannot be in balance unless your nervous system is, where your nervous system cannot be in balance unless your blood sugar is, where your blood sugar cannot be in balance unless your sleep is, where your sleep cannot be in balance unless your stress physiology is, and so on.

Everything in this body is connected to everything else, and the labs we run reflect that.

Functional labs are designed to look beneath the surface and find the imbalances that show up before disease does. Not abnormalities that flag pathology, but patterns that explain dysfunction.

Why your cortisol crashes at 3 p.m. Why your gut feels swollen no matter what you eat. Why your hormones are technically inside the standard range but the metabolites your body is producing tell a completely different story. Why a healthy plate of food leaves you bloated and tired. Why you cannot lose ten pounds no matter what you do.

These are not mysteries. They have answers. They simply require the right kind of test, read by someone trained to recognize what the conventional system is not built to see.

### TEST ONE: The DUTCH Test
*Your hormone pattern, in full.*

If you have been told your hormones are “normal” and your body is unmistakably telling you they are not it is almost certain that the hormones that have been measured on you have been measured in the wrong place, at the wrong time, in the wrong way.

This is where the DUTCH Test changes the picture.

#### What conventional hormone labs miss.
Most hormone testing in a conventional setting happens through a single blood draw, taken on whatever day you happened to be in the office. Your serum estrogen is measured at one moment, your progesterone at another. They are reported, in isolation, against a generic reference range. If the numbers fall inside the range, you are told your hormones are normal.

There are three things this approach misses.

The first is that hormones are not static. Your body produces them in rhythms. Cortisol in a daily curve that should peak in the morning and drop by evening, sex hormones in a monthly arc if you are still cycling, melatonin in a nightly wave that determines whether you sleep at all. A single blood draw catches a single moment. It cannot tell you whether the rhythm itself has collapsed.

The second is that conventional bloodwork does not measure how your body is metabolizing the hormones you produce. Estrogen, in particular, breaks down into three primary metabolites and one of them is significantly more inflammatory and proliferative than the others. Two women with identical serum estrogen levels can have completely different metabolite patterns, which means they are at completely different levels of hormonal risk. A blood test cannot see this. A DUTCH can.

The third is that conventional testing rarely captures the full adrenal picture. Cortisol is often run only once, in the morning. The DUTCH measures it across four points in the day and that curve, more than any single value, tells us what is actually happening with your stress physiology.

#### What the DUTCH actually measures.
The DUTCH (Dried Urine Test for Comprehensive Hormones) uses small dried urine samples collected at four points across one day. It gives us:

* A full picture of your sex hormones: estrogen, progesterone, testosterone and, just as importantly, their metabolites. This is what tells us how your body is breaking estrogen down, whether you are detoxifying it efficiently, and whether the pattern is contributing to symptoms like heavy or painful periods, breast tenderness, weight resistance around the hips and thighs, anxiety in the second half of your cycle, or mood disruption in perimenopause.
* Your full cortisol curve across the day, plus your DHEA. Together, these tell us where you are in the long arc of stress physiology — whether your system is still spiking and crashing, whether it has flattened from years of chronic load, whether your night cortisol is elevated and waking you at 2 a.m., whether your morning cortisol is so low that you cannot get out of bed without coffee.
* Markers that indicate how well your body is processing the hormones it is producing including melatonin metabolites and key nutrients involved in hormone metabolism (B vitamins, glutathione, oxidative stress markers).

#### What the DUTCH explains that nothing else can.
* If you wake up at 2 a.m. and can’t fall back asleep, the DUTCH often explains it.
* If your PMS or perimenopausal symptoms feel disproportionate to where you are in your cycle, the DUTCH often explains it.
* If you have done “all the things” for your hormones: the supplements, the cycle syncing, the seed cycling, the bio-identicals from your last practitioner and your body has not responded, the DUTCH almost always explains why. Because the prior interventions were generic. They were not built around what your specific hormone metabolism pattern actually requires.

#### A client story.
Lauren is 42. She has been a runner most of her adult life, eats well, lifts twice a week, has a demanding job in commercial real estate, and two kids under ten. For the last three years, her sleep has been steadily eroding. She falls asleep fine, wakes between 2 and 3 a.m., and can rarely get back down before her alarm. Her period is heavier than it used to be. Her PMS is worse. She has gained eight pounds that she cannot lose, mostly around her midsection, despite no change in how she eats or trains. Her doctor ran a TSH, a CBC, a fasting glucose, and a one-point cortisol. All normal.

On her DUTCH: cortisol curve is inverted: low in the morning, climbing through the day, peaking at night, which is precisely why she is wide awake at 2 a.m. Estrogen levels are within range, but her metabolism is pushing the proliferative pathway, the metabolite associated with heavier periods, breast tenderness, and weight resistance in the lower abdomen. Progesterone is low for her cycle phase. Melatonin metabolites are low, which means her body is not making enough of its own sleep signal. DHEA is depleted from years of running on stress hormones.

Nothing on Lauren’s standard bloodwork would catch this. Her DUTCH explains every symptom she has. Once we have it, we know exactly where to start and exactly what not to do.

### TEST TWO: GI-MAP
*What is living in your gut and what is it doing to the rest of you.*

There is a sentence I hear constantly in initial consultations. “I don’t really have gut symptoms.” She means: no bathroom emergencies, no obvious bloating, nothing diagnosed. By that measure, she is right. By a different measure, the one that asks whether the ecosystem of bacteria, fungi, parasites, and viruses living in her gut is in balance, the answer is almost always different.

Your gut is doing far more than digesting food. It is producing 70 percent of your immune system. It is making most of your serotonin. It is breaking down and clearing your hormones (yes, including the estrogen we just talked about). It is keeping inflammation regulated, blood sugar steady, and nutrient absorption efficient.

If anything is off in there and something is almost always off it does not stay confined to your gut. It rolls through every other system you have.

#### What conventional gut testing misses.
Either a stool sample to rule out infection (which is meant to catch acute, clinical-level pathogens, not the slow imbalances at the root of most chronic symptoms), or a referral to a GI specialist for endoscopy and colonoscopy, which can rule out structural disease but does very little to reveal what is actually living and growing inside the gut on a day-to-day basis.

Neither of these approaches sees the actual microbial picture: which bacteria, in what proportions, doing what. They also rarely look for opportunistic overgrowths, parasites, or fungal imbalances, three of the most common drivers of the chronic, low-grade symptoms women come to me with.

#### What the GI-MAP actually measures.
The GI-MAP (Gastrointestinal Microbial Assay Plus) is a comprehensive stool test that uses DNA-based PCR analysis to identify the organisms living in your gut. It looks for:

* **Pathogens** — bacteria, viruses, and parasites that should not be there, including H. pylori and its specific virulence factors (which matter enormously and are almost never measured in conventional testing).
* **Opportunistic overgrowths** — organisms that exist in small amounts in a healthy gut but become problematic when they grow out of proportion. This is where most chronic gut imbalance lives.
* **Beneficial bacteria** — the strains we want abundant. The GI-MAP tells us not only what is overgrown but also what is missing, which is often more important.
* **Fungal overgrowth**, including candida.
* **Markers of digestive function** — secretory IgA (your gut’s first line of immune defense), pancreatic elastase (do you have enough digestive enzymes?), beta glucuronidase (which, when elevated, signals that estrogen you thought you cleared is being reabsorbed and recirculated), and zonulin (a key marker of intestinal permeability, sometimes called “leaky gut”).
* **Inflammation markers** in the gut itself.

#### What the GI-MAP explains that nothing else can.
The bloating that has no apparent food trigger. The brain fog that arrives an hour after eating. The skin that breaks out in patterns no facialist has been able to fix. The autoimmune flares that seem to come out of nowhere. The estrogen dominance that does not respond to lifestyle interventions because the gut is recirculating it. The mood symptoms that move in lockstep with digestion. The chronic sinus issues. The histamine responses.

All of these often trace back to the gut. And the gut, more often than not, has been completely unexamined.

#### A client story.
Maria is 47, in perimenopause, busy, accomplished, careful with her food. She has been told her gut is fine because she “goes regularly.” She is also dealing with brain fog she did not have a year ago, an emerging histamine response to red wine and aged cheese (which she never used to react to), recurring sinus inflammation, and a low-grade rash on her chest that her dermatologist has tried three creams on without resolution.

Her GI-MAP shows three things at once: a moderate overgrowth of two opportunistic bacterial strains that produce histamine as a byproduct (explaining the wine, the cheese, the rash); a low secretory IgA (her gut immune system is depleted, which is why infections keep slipping through); and elevated beta-glucuronidase, which means the estrogen her body is trying to clear is being reabsorbed in the gut — directly worsening her perimenopausal symptoms.

There is no protocol on the internet that could have caught this combination. There is no way she would have found it through “trying to eat clean.” And once we have it, the entire treatment plan reorganizes itself.

### TEST THREE: FLUIDS IQ Metabolic Wellness Profile
*Three markers that reveal the hidden stress on your system.*

Some of the most useful information about your body comes from things your body is excreting. The Fluids IQ Metabolic Wellness Profile is a urine-based test that measures three specific markers and what they reveal, taken together, is one of the most underrated views into your underlying physiology I know.

This test is small. It is non-invasive. It does not get the headlines. And yet, again and again, it explains things in my clients’ bodies that no other lab has been able to.

#### What conventional labs miss.
Conventional bloodwork looks at what is in your blood at a given moment. It says very little about how your body is processing food, clearing waste, managing oxidative stress, or maintaining the basic chemistry that determines how energetic, clear-headed, or inflamed you feel.

These are not abstract questions. They are the questions whose answers determine whether you wake up rested or exhausted, whether your skin clears or breaks out, whether your mood is steady or thin, whether your digestion is calm or roiling.

#### What this test actually measures.
* **Indican**, which is a byproduct of dysbiotic bacterial activity in the small intestine. Elevated indican is a quiet but unmistakable signal that bacteria are fermenting protein in places they should not — almost always pointing to low stomach acid, sluggish digestion, or small intestinal bacterial imbalance.
* **8-OHdG** *(8-hydroxy-2’-deoxyguanosine)*, a marker of oxidative stress on your cells. Oxidative stress is what happens when your body’s antioxidant systems cannot keep up with the inflammatory load you are carrying. Elevated 8-OHdG indicates that your cells are taking damage faster than they are repairing it and is associated with aging, chronic inflammation, and the slow drift toward dysfunction we have been discussing.
* **Bile acid sulfates**, which reflect how well your liver and gallbladder are producing and managing bile. Bile is critical for digesting fats, absorbing fat soluble nutrients (A, D, E, K), and clearing toxins and excess hormones. When bile flow is sluggish, all of those processes slow with it.

#### What these three markers, together, reveal.
This test gives me a fast read on three things that quietly disrupt almost everything else: gut integrity, cellular inflammation, and detoxification capacity.

When indican is elevated, we know digestion is the place to start. Stomach acid, bitters, enzyme support, and addressing the dysbiosis upstream.

When 8-OHdG is elevated, we know the body is inflamed at the cellular level. Antioxidant support, mitochondrial care, and reducing the inputs (food, stress, environmental, emotional) that are creating the load.

When bile acid sulfates are off, we know the liver and gallbladder need direct support before we can ask the body to clear anything hormones, toxins, inflammatory byproducts efficiently.

These three markers also tell me something about sequencing. If oxidative stress is sky-high and detoxification is sluggish, we do not lead with aggressive interventions. We lead with stabilization. (More on this in Part Three.)

#### A client story.
Jenny is 39. She has gained twelve pounds over two years that won’t move. She is eating “clean” by which she means lean protein, vegetables, very little sugar, and rarely alcohol. She works out four times a week. She does not feel better. Her skin has dulled. She is irritable in a way she never used to be. Her doctor has run a thyroid panel and bloodwork; everything is in range.

Her Metabolic Wellness Profile comes back with elevated indican (her gut is not breaking protein down efficiently, which is contributing to bloating and dulling her skin), markedly elevated 8-OHdG (her cells are under significant oxidative stress, which is sapping her energy and her recovery), and low bile acid output (which is precisely why all that lean protein and those healthy fats are sitting heavy on her and why her body cannot clear what it is trying to clear).

She did not need to eat cleaner. She needed her body’s processing systems supported. Once we addressed those, and only those, her weight began to release within a few weeks, without any change to her diet.

### TEST FOUR: FLUIDS IQ Mucosal Barrier Assessment
*Whether your gut lining is holding the line.*

Inside your gut sits a microscopically thin barrier, one cell layer thick that is the single most important boundary in your body.

It is the layer that decides what gets in. What gets absorbed into your bloodstream from the food you eat, the water you drink, the microbes you carry, and the environmental compounds you encounter. When that barrier is functioning, it does its job invisibly. When it is compromised, the consequences ripple through every other system you have.

This is the most under-tested, under-explained piece of the chronic-symptom picture I see. And it is one of the most important.

#### What conventional medicine misses here.
The term “leaky gut” has become a bit of a meme in wellness circles, which is unfortunate, because the underlying physiology is real, well-documented in functional medicine literature, and central to understanding chronic inflammation, autoimmunity, food sensitivities, and skin and mood symptoms.

In a healthy gut, the cells lining the intestine are held together by tight junctions. When those junctions become permeable, through stress, dysbiosis, gluten exposure in a sensitive person, infection, certain medications, or chronic inflammation, partially digested food particles, microbial byproducts, and other compounds can pass through the gut wall and into the bloodstream. The immune system, encountering things it was not meant to encounter, responds the only way it knows how. It mounts an inflammatory response.

That response shows up everywhere. In your joints. In your skin. In your brain (the gut-brain connection is not a metaphor). In your thyroid (which is why intestinal permeability is so heavily implicated in autoimmune thyroid conditions). In your mood. In your weight.

A standard medical workup does not test for this. It will test for celiac disease, which is one extreme of one specific reactivity, and otherwise leave the question of gut barrier integrity completely unaddressed.

#### What this test actually measures.
The Mucosal Barrier Assessment is a simple saliva test that measures three things:

* **Secretory IgA (sIgA)**, the antibody your gut produces as its first line of immune defense. When sIgA is low, your gut immune system is depleted and unable to keep pathogens, opportunistic organisms, and food antigens in check. When it is high, your gut immune system is in active fight mode — also a problem, just a different one.
* **Anti-gliadin antibodies**, which reveal whether your immune system is mounting a response to gluten in your diet. This is distinct from a celiac test. A person can be non-celiac and still be producing antibodies that drive systemic inflammation every time they eat wheat.
* **Anti-candida antibodies**, which indicate whether your body is mounting an immune response to candida, meaning there is enough fungal activity that your system is treating it as a threat.

#### What this test explains that nothing else can.
Why your autoimmune labs continue to drift in the wrong direction despite your supplement protocol. Why your skin still flares. Why your inflammatory markers won’t normalize. Why the elimination diet you tried six years ago helped temporarily but you couldn’t sustain it. Why you feel inflamed in a way that has no obvious cause.

For my clients with autoimmunity in particular and given my own history, I see a great many of them, this test is non-negotiable. There is no reliable path to autoimmune stabilization without understanding the state of the gut barrier.

#### A client story.
Renee is 44. Diagnosed with Hashimoto’s three years ago. Her thyroid antibodies have refused to come down despite a clean diet, the levothyroxine her endocrinologist prescribed, and a few rounds of “anti-inflammatory” supplement protocols from other practitioners. Her energy is unreliable. Her hair has been thinning. Her digestion feels reactive — to what, she cannot pin down.

Her Mucosal Barrier Assessment comes back with elevated anti-gliadin antibodies (gluten is driving an immune response in her, even though she does not have celiac disease), suppressed sIgA (her gut immune system is exhausted, which explains why she feels reactive to everything), and elevated anti-candida antibodies (fungal activity that has been quietly fueling the fire).

Of course her antibodies were not coming down. Of course her thyroid was still inflamed. Once we addressed the inputs driving the autoimmune response, instead of just managing the downstream effects, her labs began to move in the direction they were supposed to. Slowly, sequentially, and durably.

### TEST FIVE: MRT Food Sensitivity Test
*Which foods are quietly inflaming your system.*

If you have ever done an elimination diet and felt better, then reintroduced foods and weren’t sure which ones were the problem. If you have ever cleaned up your diet on every metric you know of and your body has refused to respond. If you have ever wondered whether the “healthy” foods you are eating might somehow be working against you specifically, this test was made for that question.

It is also one of the most misunderstood tests in functional medicine, so I want to walk you through what it actually does.

#### Food allergies vs. food sensitivities - they are not the same.
A food allergy is an IgE-mediated response. It is what happens when someone eats peanuts and their throat starts to close, or has an immediate hive response to shellfish. It is dramatic, immediate, and easy to identify.

A food sensitivity is something different entirely. It is a delayed, low-grade inflammatory response, mediated by a different arm of the immune system, and it can show up anywhere from a few hours to a few days after eating the food. It does not cause anaphylaxis. It causes chronic, low-grade, system-wide inflammation: joint stiffness, brain fog, bloating, fatigue, mood disruption, sinus pressure, skin breakouts, which means it is almost impossible to identify based on how you feel after a single meal.

The standard tests for food sensitivities (the IgG tests many people are familiar with) measure antibody levels, which is not the same thing as measuring how inflamed your body actually becomes in response to a food. You can have antibodies present without an inflammatory response. You can have an inflammatory response without significant antibodies.

#### What the MRT actually measures.
This is where the MRT is different.

The MRT (Mediator Release Test) does not measure antibodies. It measures the actual inflammatory mediator response your white blood cells produce when exposed to a specific food or chemical. In other words, it does not ask *is your body producing antibodies to this food?* It asks *does your body release inflammatory mediators when it encounters this food?*

That distinction matters enormously. Inflammatory mediators: cytokines, histamine, prostaglandins, leukotrienes are the actual compounds that drive your symptoms. They are what makes you puffy, foggy, achy, bloated, irritable. The MRT measures the magnitude of that release across 170 different foods and chemicals.

What you get back is a personalized inflammation map. Foods are categorized as low-reactive (well-tolerated), moderately reactive, or highly reactive for your specific body, on your specific physiology, today.

#### What this test explains that nothing else can.
Why the “healthy” salmon and avocado you eat three times a week leaves you bloated and tired. (Healthy in general — not necessarily healthy for you.)

Why your inflammation markers won’t budge even though you eat “clean.” Why your skin flares on a schedule that does not match anything obvious. Why your joints have started aching at 45 in a way they did not at 40. Why your weight refuses to release despite a meticulous diet.

It is one of the most personalizing tests I run, because it cuts straight through the noise of generic dietary advice and tells me what this specific woman’s body is reacting to right now. And in 100 percent of cases, the list contains foods she would not have suspected. Often beloved staples. Often things considered universally healthy.

#### A client story.
Ally is 35, a marathon runner, eats almost entirely whole foods, no gluten, no dairy, mostly plant-based with high-quality fish and chicken. She works with a dietitian, tracks her macros, and is doing all of it correctly by every conventional metric. She still has chronic joint pain in her knees and hips that is worsening, persistent low-grade bloating, and a tired-but-wired energy pattern she cannot resolve.

Her MRT reveals high reactivity to chicken (which she eats almost daily), eggs (one of her primary breakfast staples), almonds (her go-to snack), and ginger (in her recovery drink after every run). Moderate reactivity to broccoli, spinach, and salmon, staples in her rotation. The foods she is eating on her dietitian’s recommendation are quietly driving the inflammation she cannot resolve.

This is not a moral failing on Ally’s part, and it is certainly not a failure of effort. It is a mismatch between a generic clean-eating template and the specific inflammatory pattern her body is carrying right now. Three weeks into rotating those foods out and bringing in her low-reactive choices, the joint pain begins to subside and the bloating lifts. She has done nothing differently in terms of trying. She has only finally been told what her body was reacting to.

## Part 3
### Why the Right Labs Are Only Half the Answer
*Safety before strategy - the piece almost every practitioner skips.*

Here is what almost happens to women who get to this point.

She reads a guide like this one. She finally has the language for what she has been living through. She knows the right tests now. She finds a practitioner who will run them. The labs come back, and for the first time in years, the imbalances are visible. She has the answers.

And then the practitioner hands her a protocol.

A long list of supplements. A restrictive diet. A multi-page plan to take on simultaneously. Sometimes a detox layered on top. Sometimes a hormone protocol stacked next to it. Sometimes a parasite cleanse running alongside both. She leaves the appointment full of hope, takes the protocol home, and starts.

For about three weeks, it goes okay. Then something starts to slide. She feels worse. She is more tired than she was before, more reactive, possibly inflamed in new ways. She thinks she must be doing it wrong. She doubles down or gives up. Either way, the protocol does not work.

This is not a failure of testing. The tests were right. She is not the one who failed.

What failed was the *sequence*.

### Safety before strategy.
This is the principle that sits at the heart of every protocol I build for my clients, and it is the principle most practitioners. Even good ones, even well meaning ones routinely skip.

Your body cannot heal in a state of physiological alarm.

If your nervous system is in chronic survival mode, your stress hormones are running the show. Cortisol is high, or high-then-flat-then-high. Your digestion is shut down. Your inflammation is up. Your detoxification pathways are sluggish. Your body, in this state, is in survival mode. It is wired to protect, not to repair.

You cannot push a body in survival mode to do deep healing work. It will not respond. It will, if you push hard enough, destabilize further. This is what is happening to the woman with the stacked protocol who keeps feeling worse.

Her practitioner gave her the right interventions, possibly in the right form and asked her body to do work it was not yet safe enough to do.

The first move, always, is safety. We have to bring the system down before we can ask it to come back up. We address the nervous system. We stabilize blood sugar. We support sleep. We lower the inflammatory load. We rebuild basic capacity.

Only then do we ask the body to begin the deeper work of repair.
